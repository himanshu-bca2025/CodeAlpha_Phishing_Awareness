1. What is Phishing?

Phishing is a highly deceptive cyber attack. Attackers disguise themselves as trusted entities (like banks, your employer, or popular websites) to manipulate you into sharing sensitive information.

  • The Goal: Stealing passwords, credit card numbers, or personal identity details.
  • The Medium: Emails, SMS (Smishing), Voice calls (Vishing), or fake social media messages.
  • The Danger: It relies on human error rather than advanced hacking, making it very dangerous.

2. Recognizing Fake Emails & Websites

Attackers create clones of real websites and emails. Here is how to spot them:

  • Check the URL: Look for typos like "amaz0n.com" or "paypaI.com" (capital i instead of L).
  • HTTPS: Ensure the site has "https://" and a padlock, though some fake sites use this too!
  • Generic Greetings: Real companies use your name. Scammers use "Dear Customer" or "Dear User".
  • Sense of Urgency: Phrases like "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" are designed to create panic.

3. Social Engineering Tactics

Social engineering targets your psychology. Scammers manipulate your emotions to bypass your logical thinking:

  • Fear & Panic: "Unauthorized login detected! Click here to secure your account."
  • Greed & Reward: "Congratulations! You have won a $500 gift card. Claim it now."
  • Curiosity: "Is this you in this photo/video?"
  • Authority: An email pretending to be from your CEO asking for an urgent wire transfer.

4. Best Practices to Protect Yourself

Keep yourself secure by developing these habits:

  • Think Before You Click: Never click links in unsolicited emails.
  • Go Direct: If your bank emails you an alert, type the bank's URL directly into your browser.
  • Enable 2FA: Two-Factor Authentication acts as a safety net even if your password is stolen.
  • Report It: Mark suspicious emails as "Spam" or "Phishing" in your email client.
